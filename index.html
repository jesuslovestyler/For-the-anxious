For the Anxious

You don't have to calm yourself before you come to Him.
Come as you are — worried, tired, and still held.

✦

If you found this page in the middle of the night, with your mind racing and your chest tight, you are not in the wrong place — and you are not a disappointment to God.

Anxiety is not a failure of faith. Jesus spoke about worry directly, at length, and with tenderness, because He knew we would carry it. He didn't speak to shame the worried. He spoke to steady them. He still does.

Read slowly. There's no hurry here. Let His words sit with you for a while.

Matthew 6:25–34Jesus on worry

On a hillside, to ordinary people who wondered where the next meal would come from, Jesus didn't say, try harder not to worry. He pointed them to their Father — and to the birds.

Therefore I tell you, don't be anxious for your life: what you will eat, or what you will drink; nor yet for your body, what you will wear. Isn't life more than food, and the body more than clothing?

See the birds of the sky, that they don't sow, neither do they reap, nor gather into barns. Your heavenly Father feeds them. Aren't you of much more value than they?

Matthew 6:25–26, World English Bible

He is not saying your needs are small. He is saying you are not small to your Father. The birds are fed without barns or striving — and you, Jesus says, are of much more value than they.

But seek first God's Kingdom, and his righteousness; and all these things will be given to you as well. Therefore don't be anxious for tomorrow, for tomorrow will be anxious for itself. Each day's own evil is sufficient.

Matthew 6:33–34, World English Bible

Just today. Not the whole future at once — just today, held in His hands. Tomorrow belongs to Him, too.

John 14:27His peace, given not as the world gives

On the night before the cross, Jesus left His friends a gift. Not advice. Not a technique. A gift — His own peace.

Peace I leave with you. My peace I give to you; not as the world gives, I give to you. Don't let your heart be troubled, neither let it be fearful.

John 14:27, World English Bible

The world gives peace only when the circumstances cooperate — when the news is good, when the bill is paid, when the scan comes back clear. Jesus gives a different kind. His peace can be present even while the storm is still there, because it rests on Him, not on the weather.

John 16:33Courage in a world with trouble

Jesus never pretended this world would be easy. He told the truth about the trouble — and then He told a greater truth about Himself.

I have told you these things, that in me you may have peace. In the world you have oppression; but cheer up! I have overcome the world.

John 16:33, World English Bible

Cheer up is not a command to pretend. It is an invitation to look at the One who has already overcome — and to borrow your courage from His victory, not from your own strength.

Philippians 4:6–7Tell God, and be guarded

Paul doesn't tell the anxious to hide their worries or dress them up. He tells them to bring every single one to God — plainly, honestly, by name.

In nothing be anxious, but in everything, by prayer and petition with thanksgiving, let your requests be made known to God. And the peace of God, which surpasses all understanding, will guard your hearts and your thoughts in Christ Jesus.

Philippians 4:6–7, World English Bible

A guard, not a grade. This peace is pictured as a guard standing watch over your heart and your thoughts. You bring the requests. He does the guarding. If your mind is still racing after you pray, you haven't failed — stay near Him, and let Him keep watch.

In everything — the big fear and the small one you feel silly mentioning. Nothing is too heavy for Him, and nothing is too small for His care.

1 Peter 5:7  ·  Isaiah 41:10He cares for you

This may be the simplest reason to bring Him your worry, and the truest: He cares for you. Not in general. You.

casting all your worries on him, because he cares for you.

1 Peter 5:7, World English Bible

Casting is not a gentle placing. It's throwing — handing over the whole weight, because your arms were never meant to carry it alone. And long before Peter wrote it, God said it to His frightened people:

Don't be afraid, for I am with you. Don't be dismayed, for I am your God. I will strengthen you. Yes, I will help you. Yes, I will uphold you with the right hand of my righteousness.

Isaiah 41:10, World English Bible

I am with you. I will strengthen you. I will help you. I will uphold you. Four promises, all resting on who He is — not on how calm you manage to feel.

Before you go

You don't have to calm yourself before you come to Jesus. You don't have to sort the worry out first, or pray it perfectly, or feel peaceful to prove you trust Him.

You cast the worry on Him because He cares for you — and the peace is His to give. It was never yours to manufacture. It is His to guard you with, in Christ Jesus, through this night and into the morning.

He is with you. He will uphold you. Rest in that, as best you can — and where you can't, He will hold you anyway.

A short prayer, if words are hard tonight

Lord Jesus, my mind won't quiet and I don't know how to fix it. I'm bringing You everything I'm afraid of — You know it all already. Guard my heart and my thoughts, as You promised. Uphold me with Your right hand. I trust that You care for me. Amen.
